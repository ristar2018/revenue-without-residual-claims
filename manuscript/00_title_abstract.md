# Revenue without residual claims: Contract design and the revenue–profit divergence in China's listed tourism firms

## Abstract

Listed tourism firms can report rising revenue while net profit falls. This paper asks where that gap forms and whether disclosed contracts help explain it. Using 24 A-share listed tourism firms over 2015–2025 (264 firm-years), 16.7% of observations with calculable growth paired rising revenue with falling net profit (15.9% among the 17 continuously disclosing firms); measured on growth rates the shares are 28.3% and 26.5%. A five-component decomposition of 225 firm-years locates the gap below the operating layer. For 13 firms observed in both subperiods, the operating-layer margin rose from 30.60% to 38.29%, while the parent-attributable net margin fell from 11.19% to −4.77%; minority interest, interest, impairment and depreciation together added 23.65 percentage points of deductions. We then hand-collect 99 contract clauses from 32 reports issued by seven firms. The notes table contains no scenic-area contract, whereas five scenic-area clauses appear only in the main text; one set of five Qujiang clauses equals 20.72% of half-year consolidated revenue. Panel estimates for 115 firm-years with continuous disclosure associate interest and impairment negatively, and depreciation positively, with net margins; the positive depreciation coefficient may reflect a common-denominator effect. We read these patterns as consistent with *revenue without residual claims*: a contractual allocation in which the operator appears to recognise revenue while holding a limited claim on incremental receipts and continuing responsibility for asset-related costs. The evidence remains associative, and the contract sample is small, so the interpretation may be treated as provisional.

**Keywords:** value capture; revenue rights; cost obligations; tourism firms; contract disclosure; China
