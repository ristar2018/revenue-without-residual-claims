*Notes.* Calculable growth denotes a firm-year for which revenue and parent-attributable net profit are both available for the current and preceding year. The two criteria differ only where the prior-year profit was negative, because a percentage change then reverses sign: of the 68 full-sample cases under the growth-rate criterion, 35 are narrowing losses in which attributable profit rose in level terms, and 7 are wider losses that the growth rate recorded as an increase. *Notes.* The identity is: parent-attributable net margin = operating-layer margin − minority interest − interest burden − impairment burden − depreciation and amortisation intensity. It closes for every firm-year to a tolerance of 1e-9. The four deductions widened by 23.65 percentage points in total, which more than offset the 7.69-point improvement in the operating layer and produced a net decline of 15.96 points. The operating layer adds back interest, impairment and depreciation and amortisation but not income tax; it is therefore not EBITDA, and it is not statutory operating profit. The 13 firms are observed in both subperiods, but the panel is unbalanced at the firm-year level: 46 and 51 firm-years are pooled within each period, so the period figures are firm-year means and not equal-weighted means of 13 firm means.
