# Cover letter (draft)

**To:** The Editor-in-Chief
**Journal:** *Tourism Management Perspectives*
**Date:** 12 November 2026
**Manuscript:** Revenue without residual claims: Contract design and the revenue–profit divergence in China's listed tourism firms

Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript above for consideration as a full-length article in *Tourism Management Perspectives*.

**What the paper asks.** Tourism research usually explains weak operator profitability through demand, seasonality or cost structure. Using 24 listed Chinese tourism firms over 2015–2025, we document a pattern that these accounts do not cover: in 16.7% of 240 firm-years with calculable growth, revenue rose while parent-attributable net profit fell (28.3% on a growth-rate measure, which counts narrowing losses as declines). We ask where in the income statement the divergence is produced, and what contractual arrangement produces it.

**What we find.** Three layers of evidence converge. (i) A five-component decomposition of parent-attributable net margin closes line by line and locates the divergence in the deduction layers below the operating line: between the 2015–2019 and 2020–2024 subperiods, the operating-layer margin of the 13 firms present throughout rose from 30.60% to 38.29%, while the parent-attributable margin fell from 11.19% to −4.77%; minority interest, interest, impairment and depreciation together absorbed 23.65 percentage points. (ii) Hand-coded evidence from 99 disclosed entrusted-management and contracting clauses in 32 reports shows that contracts allocate a fixed fee or a narrow revenue share to the operator while asset-heavy obligations remain with the operator's statements. (iii) Fixed-effect panel estimates on 115 A-level firm-years associate interest and impairment burdens with lower net margins, while depreciation intensity carries a positive coefficient — a denominator effect that a single ratio would misread as a burden.

**Why it fits the journal.** The paper speaks to the journal's readership on destination governance, tourism firm performance and the political economy of tourism development. It reframes a question that is usually posed in visitor or destination terms as a question of contract and reporting design, and it offers a measurement protocol — nominal share, two-period bridge, marginal association — that is portable to other tourism settings and to other asset-heavy service sectors.

**Originality and ethics.** The manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration by another journal. All data are drawn from public annual and semi-annual reports; the coding protocol, the clause table and the replication scripts are available and will be deposited in a public repository upon acceptance. The authors declare no competing interests. No human or animal subjects were involved.

We would be glad to respond to any queries.
